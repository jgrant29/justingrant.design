hrsimple.com | HR Compliance | Designer & Developer

Making employment law readable, and worth paying for

hrsimple was the online home of the HR Compliance Library from American Chamber of Commerce Resources: state manuals like the New York Human Resources Manual, written by employment-law firms such as Cozen O'Connor. Buyers are usually HR managers or small-business owners with one urgent question. As the sole designer and developer for eight years, I made that content usable and made paying for it an easy decision.

The buyer's real question

Nobody wants an employment-law library. They want to know if they owe overtime, or what goes in a termination letter. Before paying, they ask three things:

  • Does this cover my state, and my problem?
  • Can I trust who wrote it, and is it current?
  • Will I actually be able to find the answer once I'm in?

So the product had two jobs: make dense legal content easy to use, and answer those questions before asking for a credit card. This case follows one buyer through that path.

Step 1 · Land on an answer

Let people see it's useful before the paywall

Most buyers arrived from search, landing on a single chapter. I modeled the law as books, chapters, and sections early on, so each chapter is its own indexed page with its update date, authors, and structured data.

Visitors see the opening, the full chapter outline, and the authors before the paywall. The gate leads with a 7-day free trial, plus a phone number for people who'd rather talk.

Step 2 · Choose a state and a plan

Show everything they're buying

The product page names the state, the law firm, and the editor, and lists the manual's real chapters with free samples marked. The chapter list sits right under the plan choice, because people decide by scanning for the chapter they need.

Two plans with one clear difference: online only, or online plus the printed guide. The trial link sits under the button.

Step 3 · Check out

Four labeled steps, and access right away

Cart, details, review, payment. Discount codes go in the cart, and shipping on the printed guide is free, so there's no surprise at the end.

Online access starts as soon as payment clears, and the confirmation page links straight into the library. The book can take a few days. The answer can't.

Step 4 · Find the answer

A reader built for dense legal content

The reader is where I spent the most design time, because people come back to it every week.

  • Clear location. A breadcrumb, the chapter list on the left, and the current chapter's outline on the right.
  • Built for reading. A serif at a comfortable line length, with plain headings.
  • Forms beside the law. Each chapter's forms and policies sit next to it, so New York overtime links straight to the overtime policy template.
  • Practice apart from rules. Recommended practices are called out separately from legal requirements.
  • Favorites. Starred chapters appear on your dashboard.
Behind the scenes

Keeping the content current, and the business running

The law changes, so the content can't be static. Editors work with guidelines beside the editor, and every change is saved as a dated version with a note, so partner attorneys can see what changed. Any chapter can be marked as a free sample, which feeds Step 1.

Staff were users too. I watched how they handled orders, renewals, trials, and shipping, then rebuilt the order screens around that work: one searchable table with filters for trials and conversions, discount tracking, access status, phone orders, and shipping labels. Their workload dropped 75 percent, and the time it freed went to sales.

Accessibility as a practice

I audited every page against WCAG and tested with the NVDA screen reader. In 2021 I shipped an accessibility retrofit without a redesign: skip links, focus states, ARIA labels, and contrast fixes. I published the work in a peer-reviewed paper at ACM SIGDOC, showing that accessibility work also improves search visibility.

Where it landed

  • Paying customers. 10,000 clients across 23 state libraries, plus forms and SHRM- and HRCI-certified webinars.
  • Leaner operations. Staff workload down 75 percent, and the B2B pipeline doubled from $800K to $1.6M in one year.
  • A lasting structure. The books, chapters, and sections model ran search, the reader, favorites, and SEO for eight years without a rebuild.
  • Published research. A peer-reviewed paper at ACM SIGDOC '21 on accessibility and design-to-code practice.

These screens are a redesign based on the production Rails views. Titles, authors, and chapter names come from the published library. Chapter text is summarized, prices are omitted, and customer details are masked.
