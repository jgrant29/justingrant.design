I design how the work works.

I started out building things. For eight years I designed and coded hrsimple.com, from e-commerce for 10,000 paying clients to the staff's internal tools. Watching the staff work taught me more than any spec, and a redesign based on it cut their workload by 75 percent.

That led me to research: a master's in Human Computer Interaction at UC Santa Cruz, then classroom ethnography at McGraw Hill, enterprise discovery for 7,000 users at Indeed, and research and design for Mozilla's Common Voice and Data Collective. Now I'm VP of Service Design at JPMorgan Chase, mapping how corporate responsibility teams work across the firm.

My approach hasn't changed. I talk to the people doing the work, map the system around them, and get product, engineering, and leadership looking at the same picture before we decide what to build. Then I stay with it until it ships.

I mentor HCI grad students at UC Santa Cruz and advise an early-stage AI startup. Before design, I studied philosophy at UCLA, which may explain why I ask "why" so much.

Off the clock I'm usually in the garden. Raised beds are service design with slower feedback: you plan the system, wait a season, and find out what the soil thought. I also volunteer on Earth science field campaigns, usually carrying sampling poles or fixing a laptop full of pine needles.
