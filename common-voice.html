Fixing bad voice data before it's recorded

Mozilla Common Voice · Spontaneous Speech2024–2026

Most voice datasets are people reading sentences aloud. Spontaneous Speech asks contributors a question and records the answer in their own words and language. That data is more useful, and easier to get wrong.

Role
Lead UX researcher
Timeline
2024 – 2026
Worked with
Two engineers, product, language specialists, global contributor community
Scope
0-to-1 product: question, answer, transcribe, and tag flows
Research
30-participant study across three products
Outcome
Shipped as Spontaneous Speech 1.0 in 58 languages, growing to 62 language datasets in release 2.0.
Results

The harvest

Where bad data comes from

Two failures did the most damage. People recorded in the wrong language because nothing stopped them, and people got cut off because they didn't know when recording started.

Both looked like small UI issues, and both produced clips reviewers had to catch by hand. Preventing a bad clip costs the contributor a few seconds. Fixing one costs reviewer hours.

Research

Spontaneous Speech was part of a 30-participant study that also covered the AI assistant and the upload flow. Contributors didn't know when recording started, reviewers had almost no playback controls, and the community asked for a way to tag speech that mixes languages.

Decisions

  • Language first. The form stays locked until you pick a language, and a reminder stays on screen while you record.
  • A five-second countdown. You know when recording starts and get a moment to think. Better answers, fewer retakes.
  • Mobile-first and forgiving. Most contributors are on phones, and recording yourself is awkward, so trying again is easy.
  • A player built for transcription. A scrubber, elapsed and remaining time, and five-second skips in both directions.
  • Code-switching tags. Real speech mixes languages, like Spanish and English, so transcribers can tag phrases by language instead of labeling the whole clip.

Researching the whole pipeline

The contributor screen is the front stage. Behind it are reviewers, transcribers, and the teams training models. I judged each recommendation by its effect on the dataset, and the team built from the design system's code-first components so what shipped matched what we tested.

Where it landed

  • A new dataset. Spontaneous Speech 1.0 released in September 2025 in 58 languages. Release 2.0, in December 2025, covers 62.
  • Real speech at volume. 357+ hours of unscripted answers, the conversational audio read-aloud datasets can't provide.
  • Mixed-language speech kept. Code-switching tags mean it gets labeled, not thrown out.

Sources: Common Voice 24.0 release
